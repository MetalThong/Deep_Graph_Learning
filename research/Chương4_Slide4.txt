wordi

--- PAGE 4 ---
The Key Idea of Convolution
Suppose we have a 1D signal:
x1, x2, x3, x4, x5, x6.
Consider a kernel:
w1, w2, w3.
The kernel slides across the input.
zi = w1xi + w2xi+1 + w3xi+2
for each valid position.
Thus:
[x1, x2, x3]→z1
[x2, x3, x4]→z2
[x3, x4, x5]→z3.
The same weights are reused.
Lecturer: Dinh Que Tran ( Intelligent System Development ) CNN: From Scratch to Frameworks 4 / 36

Convolution as a Function
A convolution layer is another parameterized function.
For one-dimensional input:
Z = Conv1D(X ; W, b)
Then normally:
H = ReLU(Z ).
Therefore:
f (X ) = ReLU (Conv1D( X ; W, b))
This fits directly into our previous idea:

--- PAGE 6 ---
Why Does Convolution Help?
A convolution has two important properties.
1 Local connectivity
The kernel sees only a small neighborhood.
2 Weight sharing
The same kernel is reused at different positions.
Therefore:
CNN = Locality +Weight Sharing+Nonlinearity
This makes CNNs particularly useful for:
images
audio
time series
sequences
text

Convolution Kernel
Suppose:
X = [2,1,3,4,2].
A kernel is:
W = [0.5,−1,0.5].
At the first position:
z1 = 2(0.5) +1(−1) +3(0.5).

z1 = 1.

The same kernel is reused.

Convolution + ReLU
After convolution:
Z = Conv(X, W ).
Then:
H = ReLU(Z ).
Therefore:
H = ReLU (Conv(X, W ) + b)
This is directly analogous to:
H = ReLU(XW + b)
in a dense layer.
The major difference is the structured local operation.

--- PAGE 14 ---
Pooling
A common operation is max pooling.
For example:
[1,5,2,4]
with pool size 2 gives:
[5,4].
Mathematically:
pi = max( xi, xi+1)
Pooling reduces the representation size.
It can also make the representation less sensitive to small positional changes.
